City of Maths, Leeds · 2026 – 2027

As Event Lead & Ambassador at City of Maths, I plan, design and deliver interactive educational workshops in schools — experiences that combine play with genuine mathematical content, and make research ideas accessible to everyone.

[ in the room · click any image to enlarge ]

01

Venn cricket bowling machine — with the University of Cambridge. Hundreds of schoolchildren in Leeds built working, small-scale replicas of the historic Venn cricket bowling machine as part of the UK’s first City of Maths programme. Designed for pupils to build themselves, the machines teach that maths is about curiosity, creativity and solving real-world problems — not just numbers and equations. Pupils tested launch angles, measured distance travelled, and met the full-size replica in Leeds city centre.

[ the workshops I deliver ]

  1. 01

    Build

    Pupils assemble a mini Venn bowling machine from a kit made at Cambridge’s Department of Engineering, following step-by-step instructions.

  2. 02

    Test

    They try different launch angles to see how each one changes the shot.

  3. 03

    Measure

    They measure how far the ball travels — measurement, forces and angles, learnt by doing.

  4. 04

    Connect

    They learn about John Venn and the Venn diagrams used in classrooms, workplaces and research worldwide.

The workshops ran in mid-June, mixing maths, engineering, teamwork and scientific investigation. Afterwards the full-size replica went on show in Leeds city centre for a weekend, where visitors built and operated the minis and tried to launch squash balls into buckets.

The programme was covered by the University of Cambridge Department of Engineering, which named me among the City of Maths Ambassadors.
